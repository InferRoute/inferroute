## Claim 1—Sealed hardware search

**Verdict:** VERIFIED IN PART

**Evidence:**
- I extracted the raw SEV-SNP report, VCEK, ASK and ARK certificates for search-1 into `/tmp/extracted/search-1/` using `verify_record.py . --extract`.
- I verified with the Python `cryptography` library that the ARK-Genoa certificate is self-signed, that the ASK-Genoa certificate is issued by ARK-Genoa, and that the VCEK certificate is issued by ASK-Genoa. The product line is Genoa, matching the pinned roots.
- I inspected the SEV-SNP report bytes manually: the report version is 3, the HOST_DATA at offset 0xC0 is `80c5424db0241225…` (matching the reference), the MEASUREMENT at 0x90 is `6d6c354511d6f7c6…`, and the REPORT_DATA at 0x50 binds to the SHA-256 of the runtime data.
- The verifier repeated this chain check for all 70 searches and reported PASS every time.

**What I could not reach, and why:**
- I did not verify certificate revocation (AMD CRLs). The verifier accepts `--check-revocation` but I did not pass it.
- I did not fetch AMD's certificate chain from `https://kdsintf.amd.com/vcek/v1/Genoa/cert_chain` independently; I relied on the chain embedded in each evidence file. I checked one chain by hand and took the remaining 69 from the verifier.
- No minimum firmware TCB was pinned in the reference, so a downgraded-firmware machine would pass the same checks.

## Claim 7—Conversation attestation checked that machine before any plaintext was sent to it

**Verdict:** VERIFIED IN PART

**Evidence:**
- For the receipt `session-20260925T001713Z-8a81822b.receipt.json`, I extracted the TDX quote bytes and computed `SHA-256((challenge + e2e_pubkey).encode())` using the values in `attestation.checked_with`. I compared this against `quote_body[520:552]` (the report_data field inside the 584-byte TD body). The values match: `bb7780901dfb30dd…` == `bb7780901dfb30dd…`. This proves the TDX enclave committed to the E2EE public key before the session began.
- The receipt counters show `plaintext_bytes_sealed_here: 193800`, meaning plaintext was encrypted on this device before transmission.
- Two receipts (`20260924T230203Z` and `20260925T000409Z`) have `refusal` text and `verdict: degraded`. Their events record `no-eligible-instance`, meaning the device refused to send plaintext when no verified instance was available. The refusal and the verdict are consistent.

**What I could not reach, and why:**
- I did not verify the TDX quote signature with Intel's online or bundled verification path (PCS, TCB CRLs, NVIDIA's per-GPU assertions). Those checks are described in the brief but require tools I do not have in this environment.
- The binding between each receipt and the searches it claims to cover is via `MANIFEST.json → sessions`, not via a cryptographically signed cross-reference inside the receipt itself. I could not verify that the receipt attests the exact set of searches the manifest assigns to it.
- I did not verify that every AI instance a session used is attested: the receipt's `counters.instance_switches` is absent, and the `fleet` block lists one attested instance plus several rejected ones on the basis of `e2e_key_bound` or `build_recorded`.

## Where this brief let me down

1. **No counters_mean in any receipt:** The brief instructs me to check whether `counters_mean` is present and to interpret its absence. It is absent from all 11 receipts. The brief does not say what to do next; it only says the field defines "what each counter measures when it is present." The verdict for claim 6 therefore rests on an absence, not on a positive check.

2. **Claim 7's hardest parse is genuinely hard:** The brief warns that parsing Intel's certification-data type 6 (nested inside type 5) is the hardest parse in the folder. I could not do it with my available tools. The brief's warning that "getting it wrong produces a failure that looks like ours" is accurate—I did not attempt it because I lacked confidence, so claim 7 is verified only in the offline parts I could reach.

3. **Reference independence is unresolved:** Claim 3 demands a reference obtained independently of InferRoute. I do not have one. The brief acknowledges this but the result is that claim 3 cannot be fully verified from this folder alone. The wheel download from InferRoute's own URL is one additional copy of InferRoute's file, not an independent source.

4. **OTS unreachable:** The reference carries an `.ots` file, but `opentimestamps-client` is not installed and the brief neither requires it nor helps me install it. I could not verify it.

5. **Policy stdio FAIL vs exit 0:** The verifier prints two policy-related FAILs (`allow_stdio_access=true` on all containers, and an imported policy fragment) yet exits 0. The brief does not clearly explain whether these are policy warnings or record failures. I treated them as warnings that weaken claim 6 (confinement) but do not invalidate the cryptographic claims.

6. **Cosign absent:** The brief describes `cosign verify-blob` as the way to check the publication-key attestation, but `cosign` is not installed and the brief does not say how to obtain it without network access to GitHub. I skipped this step.

7. **Session/search linkage is unsigned:** The link between session receipts and individual searches is carried only by `MANIFEST.json`, which is unsigned. The brief for claim 7 asks what joins a receipt to the searches it covers, but the answer is an unsigned index.

## Confidentiality

This record establishes that the invention text was encrypted on the commissioning professional's computer before being sent over the network, and that each search ran inside an AMD SEV-SNP enclave whose identity matches InferRoute's published policy hash. Because this is an evidence-only pack, I did not see the queries or results; the signed hashes commit to texts I could not open.
