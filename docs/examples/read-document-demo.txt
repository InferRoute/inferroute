FICTIONAL ENGINEERING NOTES — PROBANT READING DEMO

This document is invented for trying document reading. It describes two separate
product concepts. It contains no confidential invention and makes no claim that
either concept is new, patentable, built or experimentally validated. No filing
date or priority date is supplied.

CONCEPT A: REMOVABLE LEAK SENSOR FOR A WATER FILTER

An under-sink water filter has a replaceable cartridge connected to a fixed head.
Changing the cartridge can leave a few drops around the joint even when the joint
is correctly sealed. A conventional leak alarm near the filter may then sound
during routine maintenance. Conversely, a slow leak at the joint can follow the
outside of the cartridge and miss a sensor resting on the cupboard floor.

The proposed accessory is a split collar fitted immediately below the cartridge
joint. Its two halves close around the cartridge without disconnecting the water
line. A shallow collecting channel runs around the inside of the collar. Small
openings guide water from the cartridge surface into this channel. A removable
absorbent strip sits in the channel between two conductive contacts. When the
strip becomes wet, its electrical resistance changes and a battery-powered unit
attached to the collar records a wet event.

The collar has a maintenance latch coupled to a small switch. Opening the latch
places the unit in maintenance mode. Closing it starts a settling interval rather
than immediately enabling the alarm. During that interval, the unit samples the
strip repeatedly. If the readings return to the dry range, monitoring resumes.
If they remain in the wet range beyond the interval, the unit signals that the
strip needs replacing or that the joint still needs inspection.

During normal monitoring, a wet reading must persist across several samples before
the audible alarm sounds. A separate indicator distinguishes a persistent wet
strip from a low battery. The absorbent strip can be removed through a side slot
without taking the collar off the cartridge. The collar itself can be removed and
reused when a cartridge is replaced.

The feature combination to investigate is the collecting collar, replaceable
resistive strip and latch-triggered maintenance interval. The design does not
automatically shut off water. The collecting channel may overflow during a major
leak, and detergent residue may change the strip's resistance. These are design
limitations, not measured results.

CONCEPT B: BICYCLE REAR LIGHT WITH A REMOVABLE ALIGNMENT TARGET

A bicycle rear light can be mounted on a seat post at the wrong angle. The rider
may be unable to see its beam while adjusting it, especially when the bicycle is
leaning against a wall. This concept is a mounting aid intended to make alignment
repeatable after the light has been removed for charging.

The light housing attaches to a seat-post bracket through a toothed hinge. A
thumb screw clamps the hinge at one of its indexed positions. A small removable
target clips into a recess on the back of the housing. The target includes a
folding arm that places a translucent screen at a fixed distance from a low-power
alignment emitter inside the housing. The screen carries a horizontal reference
line and two marks defining an acceptable beam position.

With the bicycle upright on level ground, the user unfolds the target and selects
alignment mode. The alignment emitter projects a spot onto the screen. The user
adjusts the toothed hinge until the spot lies between the two marks, then tightens
the thumb screw. Removing the target disables alignment mode through a mechanical
switch in the recess and restores the normal rear-light output.

The removable light housing mates with the bracket through an asymmetric locating
surface. This surface is intended to return the housing to the same orientation
after charging without moving the clamped hinge. The target is stored separately
and is not intended to stay attached during riding.

The feature combination to investigate is the indexed mounting hinge, detachable
fixed-distance alignment target, target-presence switch and repeatable locating
surface. The target provides a setup reference only. It does not certify road
visibility or compliance with bicycle-light requirements. Beam spread, bracket
wear and installation on uneven ground remain open engineering questions.
